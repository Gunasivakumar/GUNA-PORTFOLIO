Selected Deep Dives

Three projects, unpacked — the problem behind each one, the process used to work through it, and what actually changed as a result.

Rtaviya Trip

Rebuilding the Booking Flow

Rtaviya Trip needed a booking flow that felt effortless on a small screen, without hiding the details travellers actually care about.

Problem

Users dropped off mid-booking because flight and hotel options felt cluttered and hard to compare.

Process

Ran quick user interviews, mapped the flow, tested low-fi wireframes, then moved into high-fidelity screens.

Solution

Cut the flow to three clear steps, added a sticky price summary, and used progressive disclosure for filters.

Hydry Water Delivery

Turning Reordering Into a One-Tap Habit

A recurring-order product only works if reordering is faster than remembering you ran out. Hydry's first version made people rebuild their cart every time.

Problem

Repeat customers rebuilt the same order from scratch each time, and delivery status was buried two screens deep, driving support calls.

Process

Audited support tickets to find friction points, sketched a "reorder in one tap" flow, and validated it with five regular users before building it out.

Solution

Added a one-tap reorder card on the home screen and moved live order tracking to a persistent status bar visible from anywhere in the app.

Repeat Order Time−52%
Support Tickets−31%
Screens To Reorder4 → 1
Weekly Active Use+19%
Brand Identity Suite

One System Across Logo, Poster & Print

A small business came in with a logo brief and left with a consistent visual system they could reuse across posters, cards, and social without redesigning from zero each time.

Problem

Every marketing piece looked like it came from a different brand — mismatched type, colour, and spacing across the logo, posters, and visiting cards.

Process

Started with logo exploration and a tight colour and type palette, then stress-tested it across a poster layout and a print-ready visiting card.

Solution

Delivered a compact brand kit — logo lockups, a two-font pairing, and a grid — so future posters and cards could be assembled quickly and stay on-brand.
